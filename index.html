cohort.reviews

Cohort is an in-depth, long-term review platform for consumer products and services.

You write about the things you own and the services you use. You can come back and add to what you wrote later. Other members can ask you directly about it, and you can answer.

No advertising.

early beta

This is a beta. What's open for testing today is narrow, and it isn't the intended scope of the product — just what's live right now.

products Hiking and outdoor gear.
Portable and nomadic tech.
services Garages and auto repair.
Parcel relay points and courier counters.

Writing a review

There's no length limit and no fixed format. Some entries are a paragraph, some are much longer, and you can add photos or video alongside the writing. You can also attach supporting documents — a photo or PDF of a receipt, a warranty, that kind of thing.

An entry doesn't have to be finished the day you write it. You can extend the same entry later — compare a newer version of something against an older one, the way you might set this year's model against last year's. Or you can add dated notes as things happen, more like a running log than a rewritten entry — useful for a service you deal with repeatedly, where there isn't really a "version" to compare, just how it's been lately.

versioning — a product "This year's jacket lost the inner zip pocket the 2013 version had."
journaling — a service "Sept: quick, no line. Nov: same counter, new clerk, much slower."

Talking to a reviewer

If an entry doesn't answer something you need to know, you can ask the person who wrote it. For now, that exchange stays between the two of you — it isn't opened up into a wider public thread.

reader ↔ reviewer — no wider thread yet
entry — running shoes, size 49.5, 8 months "Sole held up fine, toe box felt tight from day one."

reader asks "Same size — did you size up, or return them?"

reviewer replies "Sized up half a size. Still using them, 3 months on."

One business, different people

A garage, a shop, a relay counter usually isn't uniformly good or bad — it's rarely everyone's fault, or everyone's credit. You can write an entry about the business as a whole, and separately, about a specific person you dealt with there.

example "The garage overcharged me on parts. Ask for Karim if you go — he's the one who actually knows what he's doing."
First name only. No photos, no last names. Enough to point someone in the right direction — not enough to identify or target an individual.
